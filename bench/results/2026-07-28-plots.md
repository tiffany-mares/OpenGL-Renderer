## Expectation misses (disclosed, structure intact)

- **timer_spin p99:** 7.670 ms is 726 µs above p50 where the plan expected tens of µs.
  p95 − p50 is 25 µs; the gap is confined to the last ~2% of intervals, and only 5 of
  the run's 10,000 deadlines were missed. Reproduced across both attempts (first-attempt p99
  7.256 ms), so it is a property of start-to-start measurement on this machine — OS
  scheduling jitter of the frame *start* under two live spinners — not schedule error.
  89.3% of all intervals still land in one 50 µs bin at 6.944 ms (next-largest bin is
  42× smaller).
- **sleep_for overflow:** 16 frames beyond 18 ms (max 23.5 ms) vs the expected single
  digits — 0.17% of frames, consistent with occasional double-tick wakes. The figure's
  corner note discloses them.
- **relative drift magnitude:** +5.73 s after a minute at 0.664 ms/frame, below the
  planned 0.9–1.2 ms/frame band. The rate decomposes cleanly: ~0.64 ms of per-frame
  work (swap + ~100 µs synthetic workload + render) plus only ~24 µs of mean timer wake
  overshoot on AC power. The band assumed a larger overshoot; the mechanism is the same.
- **sleep tick cluster position:** centered ~15.1 ms rather than 15.5–17.5. The short
  post-miss frame (~0.5 ms) and the following tick-wake frame sum to ~15.6 ms — one
  scheduler tick split across two intervals.

## Reading the figures

The naive `sleep_for` histogram is bimodal because no ~7 ms sleep request on stock
Windows wakes in ~7 ms: the thread wakes on the next ~15.6 ms scheduler tick. That frame
is late (the 13–17 ms cluster), the pacer counts the miss and resyncs, the next frame's
sleep request is tiny and returns almost immediately (the sub-2 ms cluster), and the
cycle repeats — 4,889 of the run's 10,000 frames missed their deadline, and essentially
nothing lands at the 6.944 ms period between the clusters. That same tick quantization
is what produces the classic comb at 15.6 ms multiples in uncapped naive frame loops.

The drift figure shows why the pacer schedules `next += period` and never `now + period`.
Both runs use the identical high-res timer; only the rescheduling rule differs. The
absolute schedule ends −0.345 ms from ideal after 60 s. The relative schedule leaks two
things into the schedule permanently, every frame: the frame's own work time (~0.64 ms
here) and the timer's wake overshoot (~24 µs mean on this machine on AC) — about
0.66 ms/frame, 5.7 s behind after a minute. And it reports `missed deadlines: 0` the
whole way, by construction: a schedule that always restarts from `now` can never observe
itself being late. Zero misses while drifting is the signature of the bug, not evidence
of health.
